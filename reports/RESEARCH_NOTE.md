# Operating performance and reinvestment in three retailers

**Question:** Does a higher free-cash-flow figure necessarily signal stronger operating cash generation?

The frozen sample uses three fiscal years from each company's annual report published in 2025. Values below are USD millions. See the [full output](SNAPSHOT.md), [raw extract](../data/financials.csv), and [source definitions](../data/sources.json).

## Findings

**Walmart:** In FY2025, revenue increased 5.07% and operating margin was 4.31%. Operating cash flow increased by 717, but capital expenditure increased by 3,177. Consequently FCF fell by 2,460 to 12,660. The arithmetic points to higher capital spending as the immediate explanation for the decline; it does not establish whether that spending will earn attractive future returns. [Annual report](https://stock.walmart.com/sec-filings/all-sec-filings/content/0000104169-25-000021/wmt-20250131.htm/wmtexhibit991fy2510-k.htm)

**Target:** FY2024 revenue declined 0.79%, while FCF increased by 661 to 4,476. Operating cash flow actually declined by 1,254; a 1,915 reduction in capex more than offset that decline. A headline FCF improvement would therefore conceal weaker operating cash generation. The comparison also follows a 53-week fiscal year, so the unadjusted revenue decline is not a clean measure of underlying demand. [Annual report](https://corporate.target.com/investors/annual/2024-annual-report/10-k-report/10-k-part-ii/item-8-financial-statements-and-supplementary-data)

**Costco:** FY2025 revenue increased 8.17%; FCF increased by 1,208 to 7,837. Operating cash flow increased by 1,996 and capex by 788. Unlike Target, the FCF increase coincided with higher operating cash generation and higher investment. This describes the cash-flow arithmetic, not the causes of the operating improvement. [Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/909832/000090983225000101/cost-20250831.htm)

## Interpretation

The useful comparison is the bridge between cash generation and investment, not a ranking by absolute FCF. Company size differs substantially. Margins help scale the figures but do not remove differences in revenue mix or capital intensity.

A next research step would decompose CFO into earnings, non-cash adjustments, and working-capital changes, then examine the purpose and timing of capital investment. That evidence is needed before describing the cash generation as sustainable or evaluating an investment thesis.

## Boundaries

These fiscal periods end on different dates. The extract is manually curated and limited to nine observations. It contains no prices, valuation, consensus expectations, forward forecasts, or evidence of investment returns. No recommendation or predictive performance is inferred from the comparison.
